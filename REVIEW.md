## In plain words

**The task.** H1 asks for x^(2^T) mod N, that is x squared T times with the remainder by N kept each time, learned from
prompt and answer pairs in one hour of training on one H100. A run certifies when every prompt is exactly right up to
T = 64, on moduli seen in training and on unseen ones.

**The entry.** One file, [`submission_stage3_fast2.py`](integration/combined_fast/submission_stage3_fast2.py) (§2).
Its model is a small digit machine whose digit tables (subtract, add, multiply-accumulate) start random and are learned
from general laws. Inside the training run it poses itself practice problems from a law book generated by a grammar,
searches for short programs of the machine's instructions that solve them, and keeps the solved ones as routines. From
those it composes a round (copy x, multiply, reduce by N) and an answer program that runs the round T times. It answers
by running that program on its learned tables.

**The evidence.** On the contest's unmodified evaluator and manifest, on one H100, it certifies on the manifest's seed
and on seven more seeds, 8 runs of 8: T = 64 on both ladders, every test split exact, trained in 759–883 s of the
3,600 (§3).

**What to rule on.** Whether a training run that searches for its own program is within the contest's rules, above all
rules 4, 7, 8 and 14 (§5). And, for the project's own bar, whether what people still chose (the instruction set, the
grammar, the search's rules, the reader and the schedule) amounts to writing the algorithm by hand.

## 1. What is claimed

The project measures itself against three bars
([OVERVIEW §4](OVERVIEW.md#4-where-it-stands-three-bars-and-the-models-at-the-frontier)):

1. **Certify H1 with learned parts: done.** Every step of the removal ladder certifies T = 64 on both ladders of the
   untouched official manifest on an H100. From step 11 on, no hand-written arithmetic runs in training or in the
   answer: every table that computes is learned from a random start, though until step 17 the datapath around those
   tables is still written by hand.
2. **Nothing a strict judge would call the hand-written algorithm: the entry is fast2, and the call is the judge's.**
   In fast2 the program the forward pass runs ("copy x, multiply, reduce", T times) is found by search inside the
   training run and kept in the model's buffers; no datapath and no menu of wirings is in the file.
   Against s3i, the earlier file to judge, four more kinds of hand-made pieces are gone. Each was first removed in a
   study of its own, whose file certified alone on the official manifest (OVERVIEW
   [§4.1](OVERVIEW.md#41-the-models-at-the-frontier) and [§4.8](OVERVIEW.md#48-variants-of-stage-3-that-go-further)):
   - the hand-picked practice problems and their order: the self-practising file, sp2, poses its own from a book of
     laws ([SELF_PRACTICE.md](integration/SELF_PRACTICE.md)), and here that book is generated by a grammar
     ([SP_ABLATION.md](integration/sp_ablation/SP_ABLATION.md) §5.2);
   - the countdown over T: the run now finds it, as a use of a generic count loop
     ([LEARNED_LOOP.md](integration/learned_loop/LEARNED_LOOP.md));
   - SEL.S and BRG.X, long division's step written as two instructions: an if block on the borrow and shifts through
     the multiply's carry digit take their place, and the run builds the step from them
     ([GENERIC_OPS.md](integration/generic_ops/GENERIC_OPS.md));
   - the clock, and the rows and programs held in Python: work budgets and buffers take their place
     ([STRICT.md](integration/strict/STRICT.md)).

   Its two weak practice checks are fixed too
   ([ABLATION_S3.md §8](integration/ABLATION_S3.md#8-fixing-the-two-weak-checks)). Still designed, knowing the task
   family: the instruction set, the grammar and its target rules, the choosing rule, the search's sizes and rules
   (several found by watching whether the round came), the count loop and T's 2-digit field, the reader, the cells'
   shapes and the schedule (§5, rule 7). An ablation on the official manifest shows what s3i's result rests on (§7);
   fast2 has no ablation of its own.
3. **A general algorithm learner: results at a tiny size, and first steps at full size.** A learner that picks its own
   practice problems invents x² mod N for x < N ≤ 7 (and ≤ 26) on a bare counting machine; unchanged, it invents 4 of 9
   other formulas, and 2 more once its solved formulas carry over ([PRACTICE.md](bar3/PRACTICE.md),
   [BREADTH.md](bar3/BREADTH.md)). Aiming one deeper search at the piece the target factors through, found from the rows
   alone, brings x³ and 10·x² from scratch ([SUBGOAL.md](bar3/scale/subgoal/SUBGOAL.md)). Frozen with that rule, it
   invents 5 of 10 formulas that none of its rules were written for, with its predictions written down before the runs
   ([HELDOUT.md](bar3/scale/heldout/HELDOUT.md)). Given a loop over x's digits, it invents x mod N in base 2 by long
   division's step, right up to nine digits ([DIGITS.md](bar3/scale/digits/DIGITS.md)). With its duplicate check made on
   each problem's own rows and its ties broken by the subgoal score, it also invents 10·x² + 1
   ([PICKS.md](bar3/scale/picks/PICKS.md)). Run on one digit place, it invents all four of stage 3's digit cells, each
   under at least one of its choosing rules; multiply-accumulate needs a deeper search aimed at its piece the same way
   ([BRIDGE.md](bar3/bridge/BRIDGE.md), [CARRY.md](bar3/bridge/CARRY.md)). At H1's size, the self-practising stage 3
   learns 8 of 11 tasks of the family after three general changes to its round's search
   ([SP_BREADTH.md](integration/sp_breadth/SP_BREADTH.md)).

Four rules are **gray** for fast2, as they were for s3i: 4, 7, 8 and 14 (§5). The write-ups flag them; they do not
argue them away. Rules 2, 9 and 11 also need a word, for its Triton kernel.

Not claimed:
- nothing was submitted to the hosted evaluator, before or after the deadline;
- the leaderboard count in [REPORT.md](REPORT.md) §0 (3 of 146 entries certified) comes from the public leaderboard at

- fast2's work budgets are set from the eight climbs measured, the largest of which uses 81% of `CLIMB_WORK`; that they

## 2. The file to judge, and the others

**[`integration/combined_fast/submission_stage3_fast2.py`](integration/combined_fast/submission_stage3_fast2.py)**,
md5 `41b9a013ae9436d7b7a53e9e594a18db` ("fast2"). 4,940 lines, 258,349 bytes (3,795 under the 256 KiB limit), 207,370
learned numbers in 15 parameter tensors, Adam. Its docstring (lines 1–8) is round 2's file's, with one pointer added to
FAST.md §9 for the budgets: it names the five pieces and the write-up of each, and says nothing of the speedups.

**What it is.** sp2, the self-practising stage-3 file, with the five variants of it that each certified alone: the
weak-check fix, the strict build, the learned loop over T, the generated law book and the generic instructions
([COMBINED.md](integration/combined_sp2/COMBINED.md) §1–§2 say where they meet). Then engineering that changes nothing
it learns ([FAST.md](integration/combined_fast/FAST.md)):
- a Triton kernel runs each row's whole instruction sequence of the search's machine in one GPU launch, step for step
  as the old machine did, on integers;
- the short sequences the search ran one instruction at a time go through the same kernel, with their machine work
  counted exactly as before;
- one walk on the GPU lists every goal state's program at once;
- room for this code comes from code the file can never run with its settings, and from the CUDA-graph path the kernel
  replaces.

That much is the fast file, [`submission_stage3_fast.py`](integration/combined_fast/submission_stage3_fast.py) (md5
`91bb30b9d131791d165025d682b3f458`), the first certified version: 8 of 8 on H100s too, with round 2's work budgets
(FAST.md §5, §8). fast2 is the fast file with its two work budgets set again from eight climbs (below): lines 7, 84
and 85 differ, and every other line is the same.

In the tiny core, which runs the climb alone on fixed rows, the climb reaches its round 7.7 times sooner (416 s against
3,206 s on a B200). It has the same 66 log lines, the same 47 searches, every unit's machine work as the same float,
and the same library, rounds and answer program (FAST.md §4); fast2's tiny core is the same again (FAST.md §9). On the
evaluator the official climb has the same 72 lines, seconds masked, in four runs on the manifest's seed: fast2's, the
fast file's, the fast file with no budgets, and round 2's file given two hours (FAST.md §5, §9; rerun on 2026-10-26
with `integration/combined_fast/checks/compare_official.py`).

**How it is built.** It is a generated file, built in two steps, and both steps rebuild their files byte for byte
(§4.3):
- [`make_fast.py`](integration/combined_fast/make_fast.py) reads round 2's file from git (commit `914952f`, md5
  asserted) and applies four groups of exact edits, each asserted to match once: 2 for the listing, 4 for the kernel,
  4 for the short sequences, 23 for room. With `--fast2` it then sets the two budgets and the docstring's pointer;
  without it, it writes the fast file;
- [`make_combined.py`](integration/combined_sp2/make_combined.py) `--round 2` builds round 2's file,
  [`submission_stage3_combined2.py`](integration/combined_sp2/submission_stage3_combined2.py) (md5
  `4e0e3f09b1ab55d1bfedbbed4fb0afc2`), from sp2 and the five certified files. It asserts every md5, and checks that
  the edit lists it reuses still rebuild their certified files (COMBINED.md §1, §8).

**How it got here** (COMBINED.md, FAST.md). Round 1's five-piece file did not certify: the hour ran out in level 4 of
the climb. Round 2 shared the acceptance setups, built counts by doubling and set its work budget from its own climb.
Its round came at 3,206 s in the tiny core, which projected past the hour on an H100, so it had no official run. Given
two hours instead of one (a research run), it learned H1 but froze 531 s past the hour. Round 3's two four-piece files,
each leaving one piece out, both certified. The fast file is round 2's file, made fast, and fast2 is the fast file with
its budgets set from its own eight climbs.

**The work budgets.** The file never reads the clock. Each search, and the climb as a whole, stops after a budget of
machine work, counted by the search's own cost model (STRICT.md §2). fast2's budgets come from STRICT.md §2's rule,
applied to the eight official climbs measured with no budget: research runs of the fast file's budget-free build, all
eight certified too (`integration/combined_fast/records/research/fast_budget0_h100_seed<N>.json`;
[FAST.md](integration/combined_fast/FAST.md) §9).
- `CLIMB_WORK` = 4.3e9 units (line 85): 1.22 times the largest climb, 3.492e9 on seed 6, the answer program's search
  included.
- `SEARCH_WORK` = 8e8 (line 84): twice the largest search, 3.913e8 on seed 3.
- The largest climb uses 81% of `CLIMB_WORK`. To reach it after the latest round seen, an answer program's search would
  have to spend 1.75 times the most any seed spent.
- A climb that spent it all would still end inside the hour: at the research runs' 0.13–0.16 s per million units,
  4.3e9 units take at most about 710 s, and such a run would freeze by about 910 s.
- fast2's eight climbs equal those budget-free runs line for line, with no work-budget stop anywhere
  (`integration/combined_fast/checks/logs/compare_fast2.log`).

The fast file kept round 2's budgets, 3.25e9 and 7e8, set from one tiny-core climb. On seeds 1, 4 and 6 its climb's
count passed `CLIMB_WORK` inside the answer program's last search level. The budget is read only between blocks of a
level, and it was passed in the last one, so it cut nothing: every certified climb of the fast file equals its
budget-free run line for line, those three apart only in the stop's label (FAST.md §8;
`integration/combined_fast/checks/logs/compare_budget0.log`). A search that passed it one block sooner would have lost
the rest of its level: that is the margin fast2 widens.

## 3. The official records, and how to check them

The official records, one JSON per evaluator run on the untouched official manifest (`h100_hard_h1.json`), and the
seed runs on that manifest with only `runtime.seeds` changed:
- **fast2:** [`combined_fast2_official.json`](integration/combined_fast/records/combined_fast2_official.json) and
  `combined_fast2_h100_seed1.json` … `seed7.json`, all in `integration/combined_fast/records/`, all on H100s. The
  fast file's are beside them, named `combined_fast_…`.
- **s3i:** [`s3i_official.json`](integration/records/s3i_official.json) and `s3i_h100_seed1.json` … `seed7.json`, in
  `integration/records/`, all on H100s.
- **Stage 1:** [`s1_official.json`](integration/records/s1_official.json); its other seeds ran on CPUs
  (`integration/records/s1_cpu_seed*.json`).
- **General v3:** [`v3k_h1_official.json`](general_lessons/fixed/records/v3k_h1_official.json); its other H1 seeds ran
  the `v3f` build on B200s ([FIXED_MODULUS.md](general_lessons/fixed/FIXED_MODULUS.md)).

Careful with one name: `runs/learned_remainder/s1_official.json` is a different, failed run (the removal ladder's
first recipe for step 1), not stage 1's.

**What a record holds.** One JSON per evaluator run: `submission` (the exact source sent, as `submission.py`),
`manifest` (as passed; the shared runner's records also name `manifest_file`), the evaluator's full `stdout`, whose
last `RESULT_JSON=` line is the evaluator's own result, `result` (that line, parsed), `gpu`, `returncode`, and for
records written by [`tools/modal_runner.py`](tools/modal_runner.py) also `submission_md5` (checked inside the
container before the run) and `runner.data_md5`, an md5 over the generated H1 folder the run read.

**One command** (standard library only, from this folder, with the competition clone next to it):

```bash
python3 tools/verify_records.py
```

For each of the 18 records above (fast2's eight, s3i's eight, stage 1's and general v3's) it prints: the md5 of the
embedded source against the file's md5 now; the manifest against the competition's `h100_hard_h1.json` (identical, or
differing only in `runtime.seeds` for the seed runs); whether the `RESULT_JSON=` line in `stdout` equals `result`; T on
both ladders; every rung's count; the three splits; the times. On 2026-10-26 all 18 checked out: md5s equal, T = 64 on
both ladders, every rung 768/768, test 9,999/9,999, ood_t and ood_n_t 10,002/10,002. Any other record:
`python3 tools/verify_records.py REC.json=FILE.py`. For example, the fast file's eight, and the self-practice files'
official records (all check out the same way):


**What to look for in fast2's log** (`stdout` of `combined_fast2_official`):
- `LR climb_starts step=563`: the climb starts, with every cell right;
- `LR climb step=629 L4 round: cost 2 calls`: the round, found at level 4 as two routine calls;
- `LR outer step=630 answer program: cost 3`: the answer program's search, the programs it found
  (`C.S[T.round<i> STA.X]`), and the climb's work so far (`3.254e+09 in all`, 76% of `CLIMB_WORK`);
- `LR round_found step=630`: the answer programs, and the four rounds they call, going into the buffers;
- `LR frozen step=829 history=[…]`: the freeze, with every phase change and its step;
- at evaluation: `ROUND` (the tournament's weights), `ROUND_SOURCE (the buffers)` (the programs as read back from the
  buffers), and `AUDIT` (how many rows of the subtraction table the count downs used, and how many of those training
  never visited: 0).

In s3i's log: `LR climb step=671 L6 round: cost 5` (the round found, as one of several equivalent spellings),
`LR frozen step=870` (the freeze), the `ROUND` line (the four programs the tournament holds, with their weights), and
the `SCHEDULE … history=[…]` line (every phase change, with its step).

Three things that look odd but are not:
- `exact_accuracy` reads 0.9999999892 on ood_t and ood_n_t in both files' official records, although
  `correct_examples` is 10,002 of 10,002. The evaluator sums float32 batch means, and the shortfall is exactly one
  float32 rounding (2⁻²⁴) on the last batch of 1,810 rows. The counts are what certification uses.
- `final_train_loss` is −0.0: after the freeze the loss is the parameters' sum times 0.0, exactly zero with the sum's
  sign.
- Training reports 759.3 s for fast2 (1,340.3 s for s3i), though the model froze at step 829 (870): the evaluator keeps
  stepping, at learning rate 0, to the file's 20,000 steps. The progress line just after the freeze (step 900) reads
  545.7 s (1,143.0 s).

## 4. How to reproduce

### 4.1 With the competition's own evaluator (one H100, no Modal)

```bash
git clone https://github.com/tilde-research/one-layer-deeper.git && cd one-layer-deeper && git checkout 87e5c26
uv venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate && uv sync
bash scripts/generate_datasets.sh        # every tier; the H1 block alone (lines 282-301) is enough
python3 ../cpu_first/tools/verify_records.py --data \
  data/generated/squaring_mod_final_variable_n_t_grouped_bidirectional_plus1k_t1_t2
mkdir -p /tmp/fast2 && cp ../cpu_first/integration/combined_fast/submission_stage3_fast2.py /tmp/fast2/submission.py
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 python -m benchmark.runner --manifest benchmark/manifests/h100_hard_h1.json \
  --submission-file /tmp/fast2/submission.py --include-structured-metrics | tee fast2_run.log
python3 ../cpu_first/tools/verify_records.py --log fast2_run.log
```

- **fast2 needs Triton and a C compiler at run time.** On Linux, `uv sync` installs the evaluator's pinned
  torch 2.12.1 and, with it, Triton 3.7.1 (both in the clone's `uv.lock`). Triton builds a small C launcher with the
  host's C compiler the first time it runs a kernel. The evaluator's own image has one (`build-essential`, in the
  clone's `modal_runner.py`, line 49), and so does our runner's (§4.2). The file checks only that Triton imports
  (`_fused`, line 948).
- **Without Triton** the file runs its search's machine one instruction at a time (`_ustep`). That gives the same
  states (FAST.md §3), but far too slowly for the hour. Round 2's file, whose same path replayed CUDA graphs, needed
  about 3,792 s of training to reach its round on an H100 (COMBINED.md §9), and the fallback has no graphs (FAST.md
  §6).
- The `--data` line should print `a6414ec9fc6a5efce6414895b52d4eb7`, the H1 folder every shared-runner record read
  (this checkout's own H1 folder gives it too).
- The copy only gives the file rule 1's name; the bytes are unchanged.
- The run takes about 20 minutes (the eight records' wall times: 1,143–1,273 s).
- `--log` compares your `RESULT_JSON=` line with `combined_fast2_official`: the certified T, every rung and the splits.
  Times will differ: the eight H100 runs trained in 759–883 s.
- The run's path is reproducible in practice. The official climb is the same 72 lines in four runs on different H100s
  (§2), and no decision reads the clock: the searches stop by work budgets, the same count on every host (STRICT.md
  §2). A slower host takes longer for the same climb.
- For s3i, copy its file instead, and compare with `--log s3i_run.log integration/records/s3i_official.json`. Its run
  took about 26 minutes (the official record: 1,557 s of wall time), and its eight H100 runs trained in 1,133–1,350 s.
  Its climb reads the wall clock (§5): a much slower host can change how far a search gets before its time limit; on
  an H100 its searches end well inside their limits (the round's took 26.6 s).

### 4.2 With our runner (Modal)

[`tools/modal_runner.py`](tools/modal_runner.py) builds the competition's image (CUDA 12.9.1, Python 3.13,
`build-essential`, `uv sync --frozen` on the clone's lock file), regenerates every dataset with
`scripts/generate_datasets.sh`, sends the file as bytes, checks its md5 in the container, and runs the untouched
manifest on an H100. It needs a Modal account, the Modal client 1.2.6, and the competition clone at
`../one-layer-deeper` (or `ONE_LAYER_DEEPER=<path>`).

## 5. The gray areas: what a judge should rule on

Each of the contest's 16 rules in turn, for fast2. "Gray" means a reasonable reader could object; the point is flagged,
not argued away. Line numbers are fast2's, and the fast file's too.
[OVERVIEW §6](OVERVIEW.md#6-rules-116-for-the-best-model) does the same for s3i, and
[STRICT.md](integration/strict/STRICT.md) §1 takes the gray points one at a time, with what the strict build changed
and why the rest stayed. Where s3i differs, the end of this section says so.

1. **One `submission.py` exporting one `Submission`: yes.** The file is sent as `submission.py`, in UTF-8. It has one
   `SUBMISSION` (line 4933): the model and optimizer factories, a token loss, batch sizes of 512 and 2,048, and a cap
   of 20,000 steps (lines 127–129). It is 258,349 bytes of the 262,144 allowed, and the contest's static source policy
   passes (§4.3).
2. **Self-contained: yes, with a word on Triton.** It imports the standard library (`itertools`, `math`, `time`,
   `zlib`, `array`, `collections`), `torch`, the public `benchmark` API and `triton` (lines 10–30). No numpy, no files,
   no network.
   - Triton is not named in the evaluator's `pyproject.toml`. It is pinned in its `uv.lock` (3.7.1) as a Linux
     dependency of the pinned torch 2.12.1, and `uv sync --frozen` installs it.
   - The import sits in a `try` (lines 24–28): without Triton the file runs the old machine, which gives the same
     states but is too slow for the hour (§4.1).
   - Triton builds a small C launcher with the host's C compiler, which the evaluator's image installs
     (`build-essential`).

   *Does a dependency pinned through torch count as a "pinned evaluator dependency"?*
3. **The participant defines the model, optimizer, loss, batch sizes and steps: yes.** Adam (`LR` = 0.05, line 41),
   which clears its moments once, in its own step, when the schedule enters phase B (`Adam`, line 4894); a scheduler
   that sets the learning rate to 0 once the model is frozen (`Schedule`, line 4910); and the loss
   (`token_training_loss`, line 4870). The answer program runs its round as many times as T says, at most 100
   (`LOOP_CAP`, line 177): recurrence and adaptive computation, which rule 3 allows.
4. **The evaluator owns the loop, data order, backward passes, clipping, cadence, seeds and deadline: gray.**
   - The evaluator is untouched: one forward, one loss and one backward a step, no extra passes, no batch reuse.
   - No decision reads the clock or the budget. `build_optimizer` (line 4928) reads only the device type, and the clock
     (`_clock`, line 1441) only stamps the log's seconds. Each search stops after `SEARCH_WORK` = 8e8 units of machine
     work, and the climb after `CLIMB_WORK` = 4.3e9 (lines 84–85), counted by the search's own cost model, the same on
     every host (STRICT.md §2).
   - But the model keeps its own schedule as Python state (`Control`, line 3790): the reader's stages, phases A–D
     (the subtraction table, the other cells, the climb, the tournament), and a freeze that sets the learning rate to 0
     once every check has held for 200 steps (`FREEZE_WIN`, line 126). That is early stopping on the model's own
     checks. The loss advances it (`control`, line 4778, called at line 4890).
   - And it keeps rows across batches, as buffers (lines 3856–3865): up to 4,096 read rows for its practice problems
     (`POOL`), 512 answered rows of the smallest T for the round's search (`ANS_POOL`), and the newest 64 answered rows
     of each count for the answer program's search (`count_rows`, `COUNT_EACH`). It draws its practice probes from them
     with its own seeded generator. The answered rows come in through the loss, which reads the labels (`keep_answers`
     and `keep_counts`, lines 4600 and 4615).

   *Is a model that keeps its own schedule and a memory of past rows, and stops its own learning, within rule 4?*
5. **At most 500,000,000 parameters; buffers count: yes.** 207,370 parameters in 15 tensors (lines 3839–3854). Every
   record counts 494,712 numbers of model state at the start: the parameters, and 287,342 numbers of buffers for the
   kept rows. The buffers that hold the answer programs grow once those are found: 336,487 buffer numbers in all in the
   local check on the fast file, with the generic instructions' official rounds (`checks/logs/local_fast.log`). There,
   a fresh model loaded with the state dict gives the same logits.
   - While it searches, the climb's working state (its routines, merges and probes) stays in Python, outside the state
     dict, as an optimizer's state would (STRICT.md §1); what the model answers with is in the buffers.

   *Should the climb's working state count?*
6. **No hard-coded weights: yes.** All 15 parameter tensors start at 0.1 times a random normal (`INIT`, line 40), and
   the buffers start empty. No `torch.load`. The file also holds the true digit tables (`Cells`, line 359), used only by
   the "designed" plumbing mode (`MODE` is "learned", line 32) and by the log's report-only "right vs the truth" counts
   (`exact_cells`, line 3732); nothing the model computes reads them.
7. **No hard-coded algorithm in the forward pass: gray, less than for s3i.**
   - The forward pass runs the answer program the training run found, such as `C.S[T.round1 STA.X]`, from the buffers,
     on one-hot digits through the learned tables (`predict_ste`, line 4310). Its rounds, such as
     `LDA.X STA.Y CLA T.add.mul.o,x,o.AXY STA.X T.red.x+xn.XN`, call routines the climb solved and kept: the generated
     multiply-accumulate of two numbers, then a long division built from the if block and the shifts. No datapath, no
     menu, no countdown, no SEL.S and no BRG.X is in the file.
   - Still designed, knowing the task family:
     - the instruction set (lines 162–177): five registers of base-10 digits with one pointer, a borrow, a multiplier
       digit and its carry; 30 instructions that read the learned tables at the pointer, move whole registers, shift
       through the carry, clear the flags, load the multiplier digit or move the pointer; loops by the register's
       length, the base and the operand's width; the if block on the borrow; and the count loop, whose stop rule is the
       old countdown's (LEARNED_LOOP.md §7);
     - the law book's grammar and its target rules (lines 2026–2043, `Draw` at 2154, `law_target` at 2259): numbers
       r + q·b from the division law, the compositions of + and ×, and targets from facts, identities and the
       learner's own routines (SP_ABLATION.md §5.2);
     - the choosing rule (`SP_RULE` = "lpca", line 52), picked after bar 3's own rules failed, by watching whether the
       round came (SELF_PRACTICE.md §6);
     - the search's sizes and rules (lines 52–83), among them seven that the generic instructions needed, each found by
       watching whether the round came: five in how the search picks and orders its probes, acceptance at four digits,
       and routines that leave the other registers alone first (GENERIC_OPS.md §7);
     - where the round and the answer program are looked for, and T's 2-digit field, which is what tells T from x
       (LEARNED_LOOP.md §6);
     - the reader (stage 1's: 24 reading hypotheses, 3 pointer shapes) and the cells' shapes (three tables).
   - A judge may say `SHL.X SHL.A` is BRG.X in two pieces. GENERIC_OPS.md §7 agrees, and notes that it is the form a
     place-by-place law gives BRG.X. A judge may also say the count loop is the countdown made into an instruction
     (LEARNED_LOOP.md §7).
   - The Triton kernel adds no algorithm. It runs the old machine's instructions, to byte-for-byte the same states
     (FAST.md §3), and every digit it writes is a lookup in the learned tables or a move (`_lock_k`, line 1013). It runs
     only the climb's searches; the answer is computed on the one-hot machine (`SteMachine`, line 1843).

   *Did the algorithm move into the instruction set, the grammar and the search's rules, and does that count as
   hard-coding it?*
8. **Final logits from the model, inside autograd, with an unbroken gradient path: yes at evaluation, with gray
   edges.**
   - At evaluation the forward runs the reader, the answer program from the buffers and the writer on one-hot digits
     through straight-through tables (`predict_ste`, line 4310, and `SteMachine`, line 1843). The tournament (`prog`,
     one logit a program) is a straight-through choice among up to four answer programs.
   - The gradient test on the fast file, whose prediction path fast2 shares line for line (combined_sp2's
     `checks/local_combined.py grad`, CPU; its output is in
     `integration/combined_fast/checks/logs/local_fast.log`): the reader and the cells at the truth, two answer
     programs over the generic instructions' official rounds, and the right tokens' logits on 2 rows of depth_t_2 as
     the objective. 14 of the 15 tensors get a nonzero gradient. The tournament's gets exactly 0, since its two programs
     give the same answer and a softmax's derivatives sum to zero (LEARNED_LOOP.md §4). The multiply tables' gradients
     are about 1e-33: damped.
   - Gray (STRICT.md §1, §3 and §5):
     - (a) in training the forward returns zero logits (line 4277), so the lessons teach, not the final logits. The
       real answer inside autograd on a training batch does not fit in an H100's 80 GB (512 rows at T = 16). On the
       batch's answer rows, where it would fit, it is exact once the round is found, so it would teach nothing
       (measured on the strict sp2 file, STRICT.md §5);
     - (b) the gradient is damped by 0.1 at each lookup (`GRAD_DAMP`, line 34) so that it does not overflow, and on the
       deepest paths it all but vanishes: about 1e-33 on the multiply tables above, and exactly 0 in float32 in some
       earlier files' tests (OVERVIEW §6, LEARNED_LOOP.md §4). The count loop's count down over 16 places makes the
       path deeper than sp2's countdown;
     - (c) the programs are a discrete result of search, kept in buffers; only the choice among them is a parameter;
     - (d) which reading hypothesis a prompt uses is decided by designed run-detection logic (stage 1's reader).
   - The kernel is outside autograd, but not on the prediction's path: it runs the climb's searches, on integers.

   *Are the final logits "produced entirely by the submitted model from its inputs and learned PyTorch state", with
   "an unbroken gradient path from the loss to the parameters responsible for the prediction"?*
9. **Everything on the GPU: yes, with a word.** The parameters, the buffers and every machine are on the GPU: the
   climb's searches (the Triton kernel, line 1013, or the old machine), training's checks (replayed from CUDA graphs)
   and the prediction's one-hot machine. There is no `.cpu()` and no numpy in the file. Python holds the bookkeeping:
   the schedule, the climb's programs as tuples, its library and BPE over program text. The host reads small results
   back from the GPU (program indices and counts) to steer the search, and traces each program's instructions from its
   text into opcode columns on the GPU. That is s3i's arrangement (OVERVIEW §6). *Does that bookkeeping count as
   computation off the GPU?*
10. **OOM or timeout fails: ok.** Neither happened on the eight runs. Their peak host memory was 13.8–14.7 GB.
11. **3,600 s of H100 training, compilation included: yes, on every seed.** The eight runs trained 759–883 s, to the
    file's 20,000-step cap, and froze by 538–641 s. Triton compiles the kernel inside the run, once for each set of its
    compile-time constants, so that time is in these figures; it was not measured apart. A climb that spent its whole
    work budget would still freeze by about 910 s (§2).
12. **A custom loss returns one finite differentiable scalar: yes.** `token_training_loss` (line 4870). It also keeps
    answered rows and advances the schedule (rule 4).
13. **Evaluation within half the training allowance; certification: yes.** Evaluation took 334–407 s of the 1,800
    allowed; T = 64 on both ladders on all eight runs, every rung 768 of 768.
14. **No data inspection, augmentation, task-specific solvers, custom training loops, participant backward passes or
    hidden training work: gray, and the main question.**
    - None of the named mechanisms: no `backward` or `grad` call, no custom autograd function, no file read, no number
      made up. No training target is computed with Python's arithmetic: every practice target is a fact, an identity
      or the learner's own routine (SELF_PRACTICE.md §2.2).
    - But the climb is a program search inside the training forward pass (`climb_unit`, line 4488, called from
      `lessons`, line 4550), one unit a step. On the official run it ran from step 563 to step 630, from about 57 s to
      about 492 s of the 759 s of training. What it found, the programs, carries from step to step in buffers.
    - Its practice problems are made from the batch rows' own digits and the generated law book, and the answer
      program's search reads answered rows that the loss kept from the labels.
    - A miner computes relations over each batch's read numbers inside the model (`relations` and `Miner`, lines
      3496–3564). Only its order and nonzero facts feed the lessons (lines 4567–4568); the others, such as
      divisibility by 2, 3 and 5, are only logged.
    - The rules call "parameter-free work" valid and allow iterative mechanisms (rule 4, and the note after the
      rules).

    *Is a search inside the forward pass, whose result carries from step to step, "hidden training work"? Are a
    grammar of laws and a rule for choosing among them a task-specific solver written as lessons?*
15. **Repeated rule-breaking: does not apply.** The write-ups flag their own gray areas, and the hosted evaluator has
    scored nothing: the one attempt, after the deadline, was refused (OVERVIEW §7).
16. **The Hard metric recorder: yes.** It is not touched.

**The questions, in the order [LEGAL_PLAN.md](LEGAL_PLAN.md) §3 asks them.** Ask the first one first: if it goes
against the entry, the whole stage-3 line is out, and nothing else here matters.
1. **Rules 8 and 14:** the climb is a program search inside the training forward pass, its result kept in buffers. The
   rules call "parameter-free work" valid and allow iterative mechanisms. Is a search whose result carries from step to
   step "hidden training work"?
2. **Rule 8:** training returns zero logits, and the damped gradient all but vanishes on the deepest paths. The batch's
   answer rows would fit through the real path, at 200 to 400 s a run, but would teach nothing once the round
   reproduces them; the whole batch does not fit in an H100 (STRICT.md §5).
3. **Rule 4:** the model keeps rows across batches, as buffers, and its own schedule: phases A–D and the freeze.
4. **Rule 7:** the instruction set, the grammar and the search's rules were chosen knowing the task family. When does
   what is left count as general?
5. **Bar 2** (the project's own bar, not a rule): of what is still designed ([LEGAL_PLAN.md](LEGAL_PLAN.md) §2 lists
   it, with a cheapest test for each), which parts would you call the algorithm?

**Where s3i differs** (OVERVIEW §6 has its table; line numbers are s3i's):

## 7. Later studies

Three studies were still running when this file was first written. All three have finished and are committed, and
later ones follow them as they land. None changes a certified file or record. fast2 is built from five of them: the
strict builds, the learned loop, the law ablation's generated book, the generic instructions and the s3i ablation's
fix.

- **The s3i ablation** ([ABLATION_S3.md](integration/ABLATION_S3.md), code and records in
  [`integration/ablation_s3/`](integration/ablation_s3/)). Each hand-made part was removed in turn and the model
  retrained on the official manifest, one H100 per arm, on seed 74 and on seed 1. The unchanged file, run beside the
  arms, replays s3i's own runs on both seeds exactly.
  - **Needed on both seeds** (without it the round is never found, each time at the same level and missing the same
    piece): SEL.S (keep or discard on the borrow); BRG.X (bring X's next digit in); the two practice problems that
    define long division's step; mac posed with the running sum; the register twins; routines.
  - **Needed only because a check was weak:** Occam's tie-break toward fewer executed steps. It kept the search off
    a wrong conditional subtraction that the weak check let through; with the check fixed, s3i certifies without it
    on both seeds.
  - **Needed on one seed of two:** the chain problem alone. Without it the reduction depends on a tie between two
    equal-cost spellings, which the search's listing order breaks.
  - **Speed only** (it still certifies on both seeds, frozen by 1,584 s at the latest): the curriculum's order
    (+277 s and +321 s on seeds 74 and 1); not re-searching solved twins (+364 s, +416 s); BPE merges (+282 s,
    +291 s; an earlier +546 s had counted a search whose time swings 0.5× to 5.5× between runs); the T = 1 filter on
    the round's answers (+82 steps on seed 74, none on seed 1).
  - **Not needed:** the search's routines-first order; the late table lessons, the wrap-around fact (`ANCHOR_WRAP`)
    among them; the step problem alone; the multiply's row problem (one seed); a round cost limit above 5 (at 4
    there is no round).
  - **How firm:** every failed arm searched its whole cost-5 reach without the missing piece, so more training time
    would not rescue it. A wider search, every program up to cost 6, found nothing for any of the six parts it
    applies to. Occam's tie-break was not a search's reach but the weak check below.
  - **Two weak checks in s3i, and a fix**
    ([ABLATION_S3.md §8](integration/ABLATION_S3.md#8-fixing-the-two-weak-checks)).
    - The reduction's check can accept a repeated subtraction that is right only for small quotients. It slips
      through when none of the check's 60 three-digit probes has a quotient above 600: about 3% of runs on H1's rows
      ([`reduce_check.py`](integration/ablation_s3/reduce_check.py)). Such a run cannot certify. None of s3i's 8
      certified seeds drew it; the step problem's arm did, on seed 74.
    - The conditional subtraction's check never tests x between N and 2N, so "x if x < N, else 0" passes it. Occam's
      tie-break is what kept s3i off that program.
    - The fix only adds the missing probes: full-size ones for the reduction, built by doubling with the learner's
      own addition, and ones between N and 2N for the conditional subtraction. It is in new copies of s3i and sp2,
      [`integration/ablation_s3/fix/`](integration/ablation_s3/fix/). Both copies certify on the official manifest
      and on their originals' seeds, 17 runs in all, and every record equals the original's field for field. The
      reduction check's 3% falls to about 10^-11. With the fix, s3i needs neither Occam's tie-break nor the step
      problem: both arms certify.
