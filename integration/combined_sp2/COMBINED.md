# Every certified variant of sp2 in one file

*Written 2026-10-25, step 2 of [LEGAL_PLAN.md](../../LEGAL_PLAN.md) §4. Folder `cpu_first/integration/combined_sp2/`.
Every input is a certified file or a study builder, read and md5-asserted, never written. Nothing committed.*

## In plain words

<!-- PLAIN -->
**What was built.** One stage-3 file, [`submission_stage3_combined.py`](submission_stage3_combined.py) (md5
`11886419`), that has all five variants of sp2 that certified on their own: the two weak checks fixed, the strict
build, the learned loop over T, the law book generated by a grammar, and the generic instructions. It is built from
the certified files by exact, asserted edits, and every study's local checks pass on it (§3). Where the pieces meet,
nothing had to give (§2), but the file is 869 bytes under the 256 KiB limit.

**Does it certify? No.** It does not reach the round. On the official H100 the hour ran out in level 4 of the climb,
in the deep probes that look for the reduction; test 7 of 9,999 (§5). In the tiny core, which trains nothing, the climb
stopped at the same point for a second reason: the strict build's budget of machine work (2.2e9 units) ran out at
4,134 s on a B200 (§4).

**Which piece costs the time.** Not one piece: two of them together. Level 3 of the climb takes sp2 225 s in the tiny
core; with g3's instructions and rules it takes 559 s, with the generated book 363 s, and with both 2,585 s. That is 2.9
times what the two costs multiplied would give. More than half of it goes into setting up acceptance checks. The
generated book poses 9 laws on each of two patterns, x + o·N and x + x·N, in 12 bindings each. g3 accepts at four
digits, where those numbers take up to 9,999 of the learner's own additions to build. And each law builds the same
probes again for itself.

**Without g3 it fits, barely.** The same file with g3's settings set back to sp2's (sp2's machine and rules; the
generated book, the fix, the learned loop and the strict budgets kept) reaches the round in the tiny core after level 5,
at 1,695 s. The learned loop then finds `C.S[T.round1 STA.X]`, right on every unseen row. But that climb uses 93% of
the strict work budget, which was set from sp2's smaller climbs. So the strict budget is a second conflict, with the
book.

**The cheapest next steps** (none run). Share one acceptance root among the laws of each pattern and binding: their
machines are identical, so the climb would not change, and that setup work falls about ninefold. Build large counts by
doubling, as the fix already does at full size. And set the work budget from the combined climb's own count, as
STRICT.md set it from sp2's. Then the tiny core again (§6).

[`make_combined.py`](make_combined.py) writes [`submission_stage3_combined.py`](submission_stage3_combined.py) from sp2
(`afc70fe9`) in six steps, every edit asserted to match the text it expects:

1. **ll, g3, book: their hunks.** These three were built from sources, not as edits of sp2, so their edits are the hunks
   of each certified file's diff against sp2, applied together: 81, 69 and 8 of them as they are. Where two of them
   change the same lines of sp2 there are 9 hand merges (`MERGES`, `SPANS`), and every hunk touching a merge must lie
   inside it: the docstring; `Setting.count` (a count loop has no size, a C block runs once); `run_items` (the C block
   and the count loop, with both pieces' helpers); `RoundMachine` (a stretch starts at its pointer, a C block's
   buffers); s3i's dead practice tasks, problems and climb (both pieces drop them; ll drops more); the round's search
   (ll: no t, the smallest T's answers as they are; g3: by calls first); and `parse`, kept although g3 dropped it,
   because the strict build reads its round text back with it.
2. **fix: make_fix_variants.py's edits for book_gen** (`BOOK_GEN`, 9 edits): sp2's fix on the generated book's own
   `Draw` and `law_target`, both of its reductions with a large quotient checked at full size.
3. **strict: make_strict.py's edits for sp2**: its 52 edits, of which 33 apply as they are, 9 are dropped with the
   code they edit (8 in s3i's climb, which ll and g3 drop; the countdown's `round_ste` call) and 10 are adapted
   (`ADAPT`) where the learned loop changed their text; then its late-lessons rewrite, its renames of the clock's
   identifiers (which also catch ll's and g3's new code: 66 `deadline`s, 6 `search_s`, 11 `_late`, 6 `Deadline`,
   3 `time.time()`), its budgets and its check that nothing of the old mechanism is left.
4. **glue** (`GLUE`, 18 edits): what the pieces need from each other once together (§2).
5. **dead code**: `sub_soft` and `bfs_multi`, which nothing calls once the pieces are together (asserted), dropped
   for room under 256 KiB, as make_ll.py and make_g.py dropped the inlined core's.
6. **checks**: the file parses and compiles, no function reads an undefined global (symtable), nothing of the clock,
   the countdown or the Python-side rows and programs is left (strict's list, plus ll's count pool), and it is
   within 256 KiB.

Before any of it, the builder replays the three edit lists it reuses: make_fix.py's must rebuild the fixed sp2
(`a92d6281`), make_fix_variants.py's the fixed book_gen (`8b701e28`, certified in ABLATION_S3.md §8), and
make_strict.py's the strict sp2 (`1a1f15fb`), byte for byte. The build is deterministic: a rebuild gives the same
bytes. `--diff STAGE` prints one step's effect (`ll,g3,book`, `fix`, `strict`, `glue`).

**The file**: `submission_stage3_combined.py`, md5 `1188641953d4f8ed436374ffbab51928`, 261,275 bytes (869 under
the limit), 4,990 lines; 207,370 learned numbers in 15 tensors (ll's: no gate) and, once an answer program is
found, about 336,000 numbers of buffers (STRICT.md's rows and programs, plus the count rows).

## 2. Where the pieces meet

Most of the five change different code: the fix changes two checks, the book the law book, g3 the machine and the
search's rules, ll the countdown and what comes after the round, strict the clock and where state is kept. Where
they meet, this is what the combined file does. None of the pieces had to give anything up.

**The learned loop and the strict build: the found programs in buffers.** The strict build keeps each round program as
the flat list of instructions both machines run. The learned loop's tournament holds answer programs instead, whose
top-level count loops run their body as many times as the data says, so they have no flat list. Here the buffers keep
each answer program's instructions with its count loops marked: `CLOOP + r`, the body's instructions, `CLEND`
(`answer_trace`). The outer machine of training's checks and the one-hot machine of the prediction are built from those
instructions (`outer_machine_ops`, `SteMachine.run_ops`), and the climb's own search builds the same instructions from
the program it tests (`outer_machine`). `round_src` keeps the programs' text and the definitions they use (the rounds
as `T.round<i>` and everything those call), read back by `parse`.

**The learned loop and the strict build: the count rows.** The learned loop keeps the answer rows of every count, for
the answer program's search, in a Python dict. That is a memory of past rows, which the strict build moved into buffers
for the other two pools. Here it is a buffer too: `count_rows` [100, 64, 4, 8] and `count_n` [100], one slot per count
(T's two digits), each count's newest rows, with the learned loop's rule for how many (`COUNT_EACH`, `COUNT_POOL`). The
search reads them in the learned loop's order. The rule for when to search again (`outer_tried`) stays the scheduler's,
as the climb's own working state does in the strict build.

**The learned loop and the strict build: the answer program's work.** The strict build counts the machine work of every
search and every acceptance. The answer program's acceptance runs on the outer machine, which nothing counted, so here
it counts its instructions as the cost model counts instructions run alone (`PRIM_STEP` + rows · `PRIM_ROW` each). The
climb's budget then covers that search too. And the climb unit has no t, no deadline and no Python-side library or
program list: its budget is `CLIMB_WORK` from the climb's start, and once the climb is done with a round, the answer
program's unit runs and puts its programs in the buffers (`set_round`).

**Generic instructions and the strict build.** The one-hot machine's run from the buffers knew routines only. g3's
rounds have if blocks (`CBEG` ... `CEND` in their instructions), so it now mixes each value a block changed with the old
one by the borrow at the block's start, exactly as the run from items does (the same code, factored as `_unless`). g3's
own deadline checks in the vocabulary's listing become work checks under strict's renames. Listing bodies does no
machine work, so they stop a listing only when the budget ran out before it. And g3 had dropped `parse`, which it never
called; the strict build needs it, so it stays.

**The learned loop and generic instructions.** Count loops (`C.S[...]`) and if blocks (`C[...]`) are told apart by their
names, and both run on every machine: the reference, the search's (count loops by `_apply_counts`, if blocks in the
lockstep), the outer machine and the one-hot machine. The round's search is the learned loop's (no t: the smallest
T's answers as they are) in g3's order (by calls first). The answer program's vocabulary takes g3's whole-register
instructions (no SEL.S, no BRG.X). One thing changes in what is found: g3's four rounds do not merge in the answer
program's search, as sp2's two did, because they leave different scratch registers on random machines. So the
tournament holds four answer programs, `C.S[T.round<i> STA.X]` for each round.

**The fix and the other pieces.** As ABLATION_S3.md §8 carried it into each: the generated book's own `Draw` and
`law_target`, with both of its large-quotient reductions (x + o·N and x + x·N) checked at full size; g3's four-digit
acceptance plus full size; strict's rename of `search_s`.

**The size.** Together the five come to about 268 KB with sp2's docstring, over the evaluator's 256 KiB. The file fits
by three things: the dead code ll and g3 already dropped (s3i's curriculum and climb; the union of the two drops),
`sub_soft` and `bfs_multi` (dead once the pieces are together), and a shorter docstring that points here. That leaves
261,275 bytes, 869 under the limit, so the file has little room for anything more.

**What only a run can show** is how the generated book, g3's rules and the strict budgets act together. The book poses
about twice sp2's candidates at each level (SP_ABLATION.md §5.2). g3's rules search each one further: one cost deeper
when all of a law's programs scratch, and acceptance at four digits (GENERIC_OPS.md §6). And the strict budgets were set
from sp2's climbs, at 1.22 times the largest seen (STRICT.md §2). §4 and §5 measure it.

## 4. The tiny core

[`core_c.py`](core_c.py) runs the file's own climb alone: its `SelfClimb` with its own settings (g3's instruction set
and rules, the generated book, the fixed checks), its work budgets as the file sets them (`CLIMB_WORK` from the climb's
start, `SEARCH_WORK` a search), on the data's rows with exact cells, as self_practice/core.py and
generic_ops/core_g.py run theirs. Once the round is found it runs the answer program's search as the file's
`outer_unit` does, on 64 rows of each of T = 1, 2, 4 and 8, and checks what it accepts on 224 rows it never saw (T
up to 64). It trains nothing, and its times do not carry over to the evaluator. One B200 each, through
[`modal_c.py`](modal_c.py); records and logs in `records/`.

**The combined file never reaches the round** (`core_combined`, the file as built). Its climb ran out of the work budget
during level 4, at 4,134 s, in the deep probes that would have found the reduction. In g3's own tiny core those probes
found it, and the round came at 2,183 s. Levels 5 and 6 then had no work left, and the climb ended at 4,146 s, 2.205e9
units, with no round. It practised every piece up to the multiply-accumulate of two numbers (`add.mul/o,x,o`, level
4), but not the reduction.

**To see which piece costs the time**, a second run (`core_book_sp2machine`) is the same file with g3's settings set
back to sp2's (`COND`, `SHIFT`, `BRING` to SEL.S, zero shifts and BRG.X, the five rules off, three-digit acceptance,
the round's search at 4M flat-first; checked locally to give libcore.py's primitives and loops exactly). That is the
generated book with the fix, the learned loop and the strict budgets, on sp2's machine. **It reaches the round after
level 5, at 1,695 s,** with the generated book's own kind of round (`LDA.X STA.Y CLA T.add.mul.o,x,o.AXY STA.X LDA.N
T.red.x+on.XA`, and three like it). The answer program's search then finds `C.S[T.round1 STA.X]` (cost 3, 70 s,
21,111 items), right on all 224 unseen rows. Its whole climb did 2.045e9 units, 93% of `CLIMB_WORK`: 1.727e9 to the
round and 3.2e8 in the answer program's search.

`g_base`, sp2's; same rows, same B200 kind; "s" is the level's seconds, the work is the file's count, which those two
did not keep):

Each piece alone stays well inside the hour. At level 3, g3's rules cost 2.5 times sp2's and the generated book 1.6
times; the two together cost 11.5 times, against the 4 times a product of the two would give. The book and g3's rules

**Where level 3's time goes.** Two of its eleven probe groups take 769 s and 818 s (2.8e8 and 2.9e8 units). The book
alone spends 76 s and 25 s on the same groups, and g3 alone 248 s and 70 s on theirs. Their searches proper took 70 s
and 60 s; most of the rest is the acceptance's setup. Every open law of a probed root gets its acceptance probes
built when the group starts (`SelfClimb.acceptors`): a Root at each verify size, its numbers made by the learner's
own additions, repeated. Those two groups hold the patterns whose count can reach 10^W, x + o·N and x + x·N, in all
12 bindings. The generated book poses 9 laws on each, against sp2's 2 on x + o·N and none on x + x·N, so 216
acceptance setups. g3's acceptance at four digits makes each count up to 9,999 additions, ten times three digits'.
[`checks/accept_cost.py`](checks/accept_cost.py) measures it on the laptop with this file's code and g3's library
(`checks/logs/accept_cost.log`):

| pattern, binding | the 9 laws' setups at three digits | at four digits |
|---|---|---|
| x + o·N, XN | 0.9 s, 2.6e5 units each | 10.5 s, 3.1e6 units each |
| x + x·N, XN | 0.7 s, 2.0e5 units each | 10.3 s, 3.0e6 units each |

At four digits a pattern's setups cost 12 to 15 times three digits'. And the 9 laws' setups on one pattern and
binding build the same machines: the inputs and random starts of lt's and red's are identical (the same check). In
the two groups, 5.5e8 units fell outside their searches: the setups' scale, since 9 laws on the 12 bindings of the two
patterns come to at most 6.5e8 at 3e6 each. And 8 rebuilds in 9 change nothing.
The B200 runs this work at about 2.6 s per million units, against 0.35 s on the laptop's CPU, which suggests it is
bound by launching many small steps rather than by arithmetic.

**What the strict budget does here.** `CLIMB_WORK` = 2.2e9 was set from sp2's climbs (1.22 times the largest seen,
STRICT.md §2). g3's and the book's climbs were never counted. The book's comes to 93% of it in the tiny core, and the
combined climb would need much more: it spent 1.0e9 on level 3 alone, and its level-4 deep probes, the reduction's,
started with 1.5e8 units left (one group of them used 1.53e8). So the budget, not the clock, ended this climb. In
the evaluator the hour came at about the same point (§5).
